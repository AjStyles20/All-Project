# P003 M8 — Candidate D Screening: 2022 Global Refined-Petroleum Shock → Nigeria Fuel/Fiscal Transmission

## Decision
**PROMISING, BUT HOLD BEFORE T4.**

Candidate D is retained as the next serious mechanism-diversity case because it is structurally different from Candidate A's wheat/food chain. It combines imported refined-petroleum exposure with Nigeria's subsidy/fiscal transmission.

## Candidate question
Did the 2022 war-related global fuel-price shock create a defensible Nigerian transmission pathway through imported refined petroleum and the gasoline-subsidy mechanism, without assuming that Nigeria benefited simply because it exports crude oil?

## Why this case is useful
Nigeria presents an economically non-trivial structure:
- crude-oil exporter;
- substantial importer of refined petroleum products;
- administered/subsidized gasoline pricing in 2022;
- higher imported gasoline costs could therefore transmit strongly into fiscal/subsidy pressure even when retail PMS prices were partly shielded.

This is a different mechanism from Candidate A and helps test whether the evidence contract handles a country that is simultaneously exposed on different sides of an energy shock.

## Evidence already supporting screening
Institutional 2022 material states that the war raised global fuel prices and that Nigeria's gasoline-subsidy cost rose sharply. World Bank material estimated foregone revenues from gasoline subsidies at roughly NGN5 trillion in 2022 and explicitly linked higher oil/imported gasoline prices to the subsidy burden.

WITS/UN Comtrade reports refined petroleum (HS271000 grouping in its summary presentation) as Nigeria's largest 2022 import category, about USD23.6bn. This is strong retrospective evidence of refined-product import exposure, but the 2022 annual value is not a pre-24-Feb cutoff exposure measure and must not be treated as one.

IMF material likewise records that higher oil prices did not translate straightforwardly into a fiscal windfall because subsidy costs and weak oil production mattered.

## Proposed chain
T1: war/global energy-price shock
→ T2: Nigerian refined-petroleum import exposure
→ T3: imported product cost + subsidy mechanism / fiscal transmission
→ T4 candidate scenario: upward pressure on subsidy/fiscal burden, conditional on crude revenue, production, exchange rate, regulated prices, import volumes and policy.

## Current edge state
T1: SUPPORTED.
T2: PROMISING but historical pre-event exposure packet not yet frozen.
T3: PROMISING / institutionally documented, but exact target and pre-cutoff evidence need reconstruction.
T4: WITHHELD pending anti-leakage pretrend and exposure work.
T5/T6: not eligible.

## Critical confounders/counterchannels
- Nigeria is a crude exporter, so higher crude prices can raise export receipts.
- Domestic crude production was weak/declining.
- subsidy design shields retail PMS prices while moving shock into public finances.
- exchange-rate conditions affect imported refined-product costs.
- oil theft/production losses and fiscal accounting complicate net effect.
- annual 2022 import data are post-event and cannot define the historical information set.

## Required next pass
1. reconstruct 2021 refined-petroleum import exposure by product/partner;
2. identify a target narrower than generic inflation—prefer subsidy/fiscal burden if data permit;
3. freeze pre-event subsidy/import context;
4. separate PMS/gasoline from broad HS2710 where possible;
5. preserve the countervailing crude-export channel;
6. define later outcome data separately from baseline inputs.

Do not promote Candidate D merely because later 2022 subsidy costs were high. The experiment must reconstruct what pathway evidence existed before observing the outcome.
