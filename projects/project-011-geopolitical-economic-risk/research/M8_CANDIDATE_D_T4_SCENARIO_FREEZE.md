# P003 M8 — Candidate D T4 Scenario Freeze

## Status
**T4 SCENARIO FROZEN BEFORE DETAILED POST-SHOCK OUTCOME SCORING.**

## Replay mode
R2 retrospective reconstruction unless the exact trade-data vintage can later be demonstrated as available by the 24-Feb-2022 boundary.

## Frozen target
Nigeria's **PMS subsidy / fiscal burden**, not general CPI inflation and not retail PMS price.

## Frozen directional scenario
> Following a material external increase in imported refined-fuel/PMS costs, Nigeria faces **upward pressure on the fiscal burden of maintaining regulated PMS prices over the subsequent months**, conditional on the regulated-price/subsidy regime remaining in place and unless the pressure is sufficiently offset by higher net crude-oil revenue, stronger crude production, favourable exchange-rate movements, lower import/consumption volumes, or policy change.

## Horizon
Broad subsequent-months horizon; no precise month, magnitude, probability, or point estimate is claimed.

## Why this target
Pre-event institutional evidence already documents the mechanism:
imported fuel cost above regulated PMS price → implicit subsidy/under-recovery → NNPC deductions from Federation Account oil revenues.

The target therefore measures the channel that the pre-event mechanism actually supports. It does not assume immediate retail-price pass-through because regulation/subsidy can shield the pump price while moving the shock into public finances.

## Pre-event anchors
- 2021 implicit subsidy estimated around NGN1.912tn.
- estimated PMS delivery cost around NGN233/litre versus regulated retail price around NGN167/litre.
- subsidy cost borne through NNPC deductions from Federation Account oil revenues.
- pre-event IMF fiscal analysis already warned that implicit fuel subsidies weakened the fiscal benefit of higher oil prices.
- retrospective 2021 trade reconstruction shows very large non-crude petroleum-product imports, but exact cutoff availability remains unresolved.

## Counterchannels frozen before outcome scoring
1. higher crude prices may increase gross oil/export revenue;
2. weak crude production can reduce that benefit;
3. FX movements change naira import cost;
4. regulated-price/subsidy policy may change;
5. import and consumption volumes may change;
6. product composition within broad refined-petroleum trade data matters;
7. subsidy accounting, NNPC deductions, smuggling/leakage and fiscal treatment may affect measured burden.

## Prohibited interpretations
This scenario does NOT claim:
- the war exclusively caused Nigeria's 2022 fiscal deterioration;
- retail PMS prices must rise;
- all HS2710 imports are PMS;
- a numerical subsidy amount;
- a probability of fiscal deterioration;
- that higher crude prices provide no benefit;
- T5 model-estimate or T6 calibrated-forecast eligibility.

## Outcome scoring rule
Later observations may be used only to classify the frozen directional scenario as CONSISTENT, INCONSISTENT or INDETERMINATE and to document counterevidence.

Later observations must not be used to rewrite this scenario or back-fill a missing pre-event edge.
