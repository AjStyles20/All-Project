# P003 M8 — Candidate C Policy/FX-Access Shock Screening Pass 001

## Candidate
Nigeria's October 2023 removal of official foreign-exchange access restrictions on 43 import categories.

## Why screened
This is a policy/transmission mechanism distinct from Candidate A (origin-product shock) and Candidate B (shipping-route shock). It offers an unusually explicit Nigerian policy edge.

## Authoritative event anchor
CBN records that on 12 October 2023 it lifted the restriction on access to foreign exchange for importing 43 categories. CBN also states that the earlier rule restricted access to official-market FX rather than constituting an import ban.

The affected list included multiple consumer/intermediate categories, including rice, vegetable oils, meat products, poultry, fish, steel products, textiles, plastics/rubber, soap/cosmetics and tomato products.

## Candidate chain
T1: policy change (restriction removal)
→ T2: affected import categories/exposure
→ T3: FX-access/import-cost channel
→ T4: conditional local price/import scenario
→ T5/T6 only if a separately validated quantitative model exists.

## Critical anti-overclaim rule
CBN's stated expectation that removing restrictions would reduce parallel-market pressure and benefit prices is a policy rationale/expectation, not independent proof of the realized downstream effect.

The experiment must not use the policy issuer's expected effect as the outcome answer key.

## Current assessment
T1: VERIFIED/strongly anchorable.
T2: potentially strong because affected categories are explicitly enumerated.
T3: mechanism is institutionally documented, but realized category-specific transmission still requires independent evidence.
T4+: HOLD.

## Main confounding problem
The policy sits within the broader 2023 FX-market reform/unification period. Exchange-rate repricing, liquidity conditions, import supply, tariffs/prohibitions and domestic production can all affect prices/imports. A post-policy price change cannot be attributed to this single measure without a stronger design.

## Admission path
Prefer a bounded product/category with:
1. explicit inclusion among the 43 categories;
2. observable pre/post import exposure under consistent HS definitions;
3. independent domestic price or producer/input outcome;
4. clear treatment of other import prohibitions/tariffs;
5. time-respecting policy and data vintages;
6. no selection based on favorable outcome direction.

Candidate C should be treated initially as a policy-mechanism case, not a causal impact estimate.
