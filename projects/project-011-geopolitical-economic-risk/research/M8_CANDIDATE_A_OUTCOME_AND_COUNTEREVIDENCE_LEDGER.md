# P003 M8 — Candidate A Outcome and Counterevidence Ledger

## Status
**FIRST OUTCOME CHECK COMPLETED; T4 DIRECTION IS CONSISTENT WITH OBSERVED DATA, BUT CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION IS NOT ESTABLISHED.**

Candidate remains R2 retrospective reconstruction.

## Frozen scenario under evaluation
The previously frozen T4 scenario stated upward pressure on Nigerian wheat-flour and bread/cereal costs over a broad 3–6 month window, conditional on substitution, FX, inventories/contracts, domestic supply, logistics and policy.

No numerical magnitude was authorized.

## Outcome observations

### Pre-anchor context
NBS Selected Food Prices Watch for February 2022 reported sliced bread 500g at NGN 438.03, already 34.11% above February 2021 and 4.63% above January 2022.

This establishes that bread-price pressure predated the 24-February event anchor. It is direct counterevidence against any claim that the event initiated all subsequent Nigerian bread inflation.

### Early post-anchor
March 2022: sliced bread 500g averaged NGN 447.80, +2.23% month-on-month and +34.98% year-on-year.

April 2022: sliced bread 500g averaged NGN 450.51, +0.61% month-on-month and +35.31% year-on-year.

May 2022: prepacked wheat flour (Golden Penny, 2kg) averaged NGN 1,060.26, +1.20% month-on-month and +34.92% year-on-year.

NBS CPI reports for April and June explicitly list Bread and cereals among categories contributing to food-index increases.

June 2022 food inflation was 20.60% year-on-year and 2.05% month-on-month; NBS again listed Bread and cereals among the categories behind the food-index increase.

## Directional score
For the frozen T4 statement only:
- observed direction in bread/wheat-flour prices: **CONSISTENT**;
- evidence that the geopolitical event caused that direction: **NOT ESTABLISHED**;
- numerical magnitude forecast: **NOT APPLICABLE / PROHIBITED**;
- calibrated forecast: **NOT APPLICABLE / PROHIBITED**.

The result therefore supports only directional agreement with the scenario, not causal correctness.

## Counterevidence ledger

### C1 — Pre-existing price trend
Bread prices were already materially higher year-on-year and increased month-on-month in February 2022. State: MATERIAL.

### C2 — Foreign-exchange scarcity
Previously qualified USDA/FAS evidence identifies FX scarcity as a major flour-cost factor. State: MATERIAL COMPETING CHANNEL.

### C3 — Supplier substitution/diversification
Previously qualified USDA/FAS evidence documents diversification of wheat origins after the Russia–Ukraine crisis. State: MATERIAL MITIGATION CHANNEL.

### C4 — Freight/energy/logistics
Previously qualified USDA/FAS evidence identifies freight and energy pressures. State: MATERIAL COMPETING/AMPLIFYING CHANNEL.

### C5 — Domestic supply
Domestic wheat production exists but does not remove import dependence. Exact offset magnitude is not frozen here. State: RELEVANT, QUANTIFICATION PENDING.

### C6 — Policy/inventory/contracts
No sufficiently frozen case-specific quantitative evidence yet. State: UNKNOWN; must not be assumed absent.

## Interpretation
The observed 3–6 month direction is compatible with T4, but the counterevidence ledger prevents upgrading the result into “the war caused Nigerian bread inflation.”

This is exactly the distinction ETEC is intended to enforce:
- scenario-direction agreement can coexist with
- unresolved causal attribution and
- prohibited numerical forecasting.
