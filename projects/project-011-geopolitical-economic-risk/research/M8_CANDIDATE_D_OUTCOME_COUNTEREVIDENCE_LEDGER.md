# P003 M8 — Candidate D Post-Boundary Outcome and Counterevidence Ledger

## Status
**FROZEN T4 SCENARIO SCORED: CONSISTENT. CAUSAL ATTRIBUTION NOT ESTABLISHED.**

## Frozen scenario being evaluated
Following a material external increase in imported refined-fuel/PMS costs, Nigeria faces upward pressure on the fiscal burden of maintaining regulated PMS prices over subsequent months, conditional on the subsidy regime remaining in place and unless offset by higher net crude revenue, stronger production, favourable FX, lower volumes, or policy change.

## Later outcome evidence — kept separate from baseline input

### O1 — World Bank Public Finance Review
World Bank analysis based on FAAC/NNPC data reports:
- PMS subsidy deductions of about NGN1.43tn in 2021;
- about NGN948bn deducted in Jan-Apr 2022;
- the Federation Account received no NNPC revenue during the first four months of 2022;
- at May 2022 global petrol prices, official FX and reported volumes, monthly subsidy could exceed NGN550bn;
- the benchmark petrol price rose from below USD200/metric ton in April 2020 to above USD1,040/metric ton by March 2022.

This is strongly directionally consistent with the frozen subsidy/fiscal-pressure scenario.

### O2 — Federal Government budget statement
The October 2022 budget presentation states:
- the 2022 budget was amended partly because of the significant increase in fuel subsidy;
- despite higher oil prices, oil revenue was below target because of production shortfalls and high petrol-subsidy cost;
- NGN1.59tn was spent on fuel subsidy in Jan-Jun 2022;
- crude output averaged about 1.30mbpd by June.

This supports both the frozen directional scenario and a predeclared counterchannel: weak production reduced the offsetting crude-revenue benefit.

### O3 — IMF 2022 mission / 2023 Article IV
IMF later estimated the 2022 general-government deficit at about 6.2% of GDP, mainly due to fuel-subsidy costs, notwithstanding higher oil prices. IMF tables put fuel subsidies around 2.2% of GDP in 2022 versus 1.1% in 2021.

This is directionally consistent with the frozen scenario, while remaining an aggregate fiscal observation rather than a causal decomposition of the war shock.

## Counterevidence / complications

### C1 — Pre-existing subsidy burden
The mechanism and fiscal burden predated 24-Feb-2022. IMF's pre-event analysis estimated a substantial 2021 implicit subsidy. Therefore the post-boundary increase cannot be interpreted as a new mechanism created by the war.

### C2 — Global petrol prices were already rising
World Bank's later review shows benchmark petrol prices rising long before the invasion. The February event is not a clean start date for the energy-price trend.

### C3 — Crude-price benefit existed
Higher oil prices improved Nigeria's terms of trade and could have raised gross oil revenue. This is a real countervailing channel, not noise.

### C4 — Production shortfalls/oil theft
Weak production materially limited the crude-revenue benefit and independently worsened fiscal outcomes.

### C5 — FX and volume assumptions
Naira import cost depends on exchange rate and imported/consumed volume. Subsidy estimates are sensitive to these variables.

### C6 — Accounting uncertainty
IMF later noted uncertainty around NNPC financial commitments, write-offs, consumption volumes and subsidy costing. Outcome measurement is therefore not perfectly clean.

## Directional score
**CONSISTENT**

Rationale: multiple later institutional observations show substantially larger PMS subsidy/fiscal pressure during 2022 while the regulated-price regime remained in place, including very large subsidy deductions/costs and limited fiscal oil transfers. This matches the pre-frozen direction.

## Causal score
**NOT ESTABLISHED / PROHIBITED**

The evidence does not identify the Russia-Ukraine war as the exclusive or quantitatively separable cause. The subsidy mechanism predated the event; global petrol prices had already risen; production, FX, volumes, oil theft, accounting and policy all matter.

## Numerical forecast score
**NOT APPLICABLE / PROHIBITED**

T5/T6 were never satisfied. Later observed subsidy numbers are outcomes, not evidence that the pre-event system could have forecast their magnitude.
