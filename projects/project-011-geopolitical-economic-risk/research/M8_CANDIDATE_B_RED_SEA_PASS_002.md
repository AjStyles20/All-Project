# P003 M8 — Candidate B Red Sea Pass 002

## Status
**HOLD — GLOBAL/REGIONAL DISRUPTION ESTABLISHED; NIGERIA-SPECIFIC ROUTE/PRODUCT EXPOSURE STILL INSUFFICIENT.**

## Event and mechanism anchor
The Red Sea/Suez disruption is suitable as a different mechanism family from Candidate A because it is primarily a transport-route/logistics shock rather than a direct origin-product shock.

Authoritative/institutional evidence establishes:
- attacks on commercial shipping in the Red Sea from late 2023;
- widespread rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope;
- longer voyages and increased shipping costs;
- substantial decline in Suez/Red Sea traffic in early 2024;
- UNCTAD concern about higher freight costs and possible consumer-price transmission, especially for vulnerable/import-dependent economies.

## Nigeria-specific screening
A World Bank Nigeria Development Update (June 2024) identifies high freight costs and port inefficiencies as constraints on Nigerian trade and reports that Nigeria's freight costs were substantially above peers. This establishes that freight/logistics conditions are economically material in Nigeria.

However, it does **not** establish the missing Candidate B edge:
**Red Sea rerouting → specific Nigeria-bound product/route exposure → measurable domestic transmission.**

Generic Nigerian freight vulnerability cannot substitute for event-specific exposure.

## Transition state
T1 event verification: SUPPORTED/anchorable.
T2 Nigeria exposure to the Red Sea disruption: INSUFFICIENT at current evidence level.
T3+: WITHHELD.

Therefore Candidate B currently behaves as a useful missing-link case, but it must not be relabeled a negative control merely because T2 is unresolved.

## Why not infer exposure from geography
Nigeria trades with Europe, Asia and other regions, but trade-partner existence does not prove that the affected shipments used the Red Sea/Suez route. Route assignment requires route/shipping evidence or a defensible product-origin-route mapping.

Likewise, a rise in Nigerian freight or consumer prices after November 2023 cannot retrospectively prove Red Sea exposure.

## Admission requirements
Candidate B advances only if we can freeze at least one of:
1. Nigeria-bound shipment/route evidence explicitly affected by Red Sea rerouting;
2. a defensible origin-product-route mapping showing the affected corridor for a declared Nigeria import;
3. authoritative carrier/port/customs evidence connecting the disruption to Nigerian cargo;
4. a predeclared quantitative shipping/freight exposure measure with historical availability metadata.

If none can be obtained, Candidate B remains an upstream/missing-link case rather than a downstream historical warning case.

## Portfolio value even if rejected
Rejection is scientifically useful: it demonstrates that a globally important logistics shock plus generic Nigerian freight vulnerability is still insufficient for a Nigeria-specific downstream claim.

That result should be retained rather than repaired with inference.
