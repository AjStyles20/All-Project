# P012 — Pass 003: Weakness-Specific Training and Transfer Final Gate

## Decision

**KILL as a research-novelty/FYP candidate. PARK as an optional engineering/product project.**

## Corrected project origin

P012 is a configurable simulated audience for speeches, presentations, interviews and other high-pressure communication situations. Users can create audience/interviewer settings, practise repeatedly, receive questions and feedback, and potentially build confidence and communication skill. Clinical treatment claims are outside scope unless separately validated.

## What earlier passes already eliminated

Pass 001 rejected novelty claims around virtual audiences, AI interviewers, presentation simulators, configurable personas, multi-persona panels, audience questions, generic confidence training and speech analytics.

Pass 002 further rejected novelty around graded exposure, audience hostility/support, audience size, physiological adaptation, generic adaptive difficulty, adaptive interview questioning, performance-contingent coaching and transfer evaluation by itself.

The final provisional hypothesis was:

**H6 — Transfer-Constrained Stressor Matching:** a communication-practice system that selects audience stressors from observed performance deficits may improve performance on unseen high-pressure speaking scenarios relative to static, self-selected and generic difficulty-adaptive audiences.

## Pass 003 direct-collision findings

### 1. Commercial AI roleplay systems now close the weakness-to-practice loop

Yoodli's current product already provides:
- structured roleplay practice;
- personalized feedback on specific improvement areas;
- recommended practice exercises;
- guided repeated practice cycles;
- identification of skill gaps;
- adaptive learning loops that change based on performance and learner input;
- scenario customization for high-pressure interactions.

This creates a direct product-level collision with the core mechanism "identify weakness -> prescribe targeted practice -> repeat -> reassess".

Sources:
- https://yoodli.ai/platform/ai-feedback
- https://support.yoodli.ai/en/articles/12525502-loops-continuous-personalized-learning
- https://support.yoodli.ai/en/articles/9628260-customizing-practice

VirtualSpeech also offers personalized post-roleplay coaching, identifies areas for improvement, recommends next steps, and supports focused micro-practice across interviews, pitches and difficult conversations.

Source:
- https://virtualspeech.com/ai-coach

### 2. Adaptive, performance-driven simulated interlocutors are established academically

AI-enhanced virtual-patient systems already use adaptive feedback, performance-driven behavioral adjustment and structured communication-skills practice. Recent reviews describe closed-loop systems that evaluate learner input and dynamically adjust simulated-patient attitude, dissatisfaction and response style.

Sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13067467/
- https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/15/22/11917

### 3. Targeted feedback and transfer beyond simulation are also established

Communication-training research has already demonstrated targeted feedback on specific communication behaviors and measured transfer from avatar/simulation training into encounters with standardized or real people. Public-speaking studies have also tested generalization to larger audiences and real-world/Zoom presentation settings.

Sources:
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9298842/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37862574/
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37633990/

### 4. Stress inoculation and deliberate practice predate the proposed AI mechanism

Stress inoculation training for speech anxiety dates back decades and explicitly includes skill acquisition, rehearsal, application and transfer to real life. Deliberate-practice approaches to communication training are also established across professional domains.

Sources:
- https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7410573/
- https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0011000088161005
- https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2405452623000599

## Why H6 does not survive the final gate

The exact conjunction "observable weakness inference + automatically selected audience stressor + unseen transfer benchmark" was not found as a single canonical study during the search. However, the mechanism's individual and paired components are now mature, and current commercial systems already implement the practical training loop that H6 was intended to distinguish.

Narrowing further to a particular stressor taxonomy, transfer protocol, persona configuration or metric would risk manufacturing novelty rather than uncovering a substantively new research problem.

Therefore the absence of an exact-wording collision is insufficient to justify a novelty claim.

## Research verdict

**H6 — KILLED as portfolio-level research novelty.**

P012 should not compete for the final-year research shortlist on the basis of adaptive virtual audience training, personalized weakness-based practice, or transfer evaluation.

## Engineering/product value remains high

P012 remains a strong buildable product. A practical implementation could still include:
- presentation and speech rehearsal;
- interview and panel simulation;
- configurable audience personas and demeanor;
- document-grounded questions;
- interruptions and skeptical follow-ups;
- personalized feedback;
- repeat-practice journeys;
- confidence tracking;
- lightweight 2D/3D avatars;
- optional future VR support.

This would be valuable as portfolio/product engineering even though it is no longer retained as a strong research-novelty candidate.

## Safety and scope boundary

The system may support communication practice and confidence-building, but should not claim to diagnose or treat social anxiety, stage-fright disorders or other mental-health conditions without appropriate clinical design and validation.

## Final status

**P012: KILL as research-novelty/FYP candidate; PARK as optional engineering/product project.**

No formal research specification or implementation is authorized under the research-shortlist workflow.
