# P003 M8 — Candidate C Crude Palm-Oil Exposure Pass

## Status
**T2 RETROSPECTIVELY SUPPORTED FOR A NARROW HS LINE; STRICT CUTOFF-SAFE STATUS NOT YET ESTABLISHED.**

## Narrowed economic identifier
Candidate C is narrowed from generic "vegetable oils" to **HS 151110 — crude palm oil** for the first exposure test.

This is not yet the full Candidate C basket.

## Legal screen
Nigeria's Trade Information Portal states that refined vegetable oils/fats are prohibited while crude vegetable oil is not banned. This makes crude palm oil a materially cleaner legal-import candidate than an undifferentiated refined-oil basket.

## 2023 import exposure
World Bank WITS/UN Comtrade reports Nigeria's 2023 imports of HS 151110 crude palm oil at approximately USD 59.374 million.

Reported partners include:
- Malaysia: USD 50.385 million;
- Côte d'Ivoire: USD 8.638 million;
- Indonesia: USD 0.156 million;
- China, Cameroon and Singapore at much smaller values.

This establishes actual Nigerian crude-palm-oil import exposure in the calendar year of the policy change.

## Independent sector context
USDA/FAS's April 2023 Nigeria Oilseeds and Products Annual states that Nigeria relied on imports to bridge the palm-oil supply gap and expected substantial palm-oil supply requirements. Its April 2024 follow-up states that, despite the October 2023 lifting of FX restrictions, importers continued to report difficulty obtaining foreign currency.

That latter observation is material counterevidence against any simplistic chain:
restriction removed → FX readily available → imports cheaper/more available.

## Transition implications
T1 policy event: VERIFIED.
T2 narrow crude-palm-oil import exposure: **SUPPORTED in retrospective/R2 sense**.
T2 strict R1/cutoff-safe: NOT ESTABLISHED because the exact 2023 annual trade observation and its availability/vintage at the 12-Oct-2023 boundary are not yet demonstrated.
T3 FX-access/landed-cost transmission: HOLD.
T4+: WITHHELD.

## Important limitations
1. Annual 2023 trade data straddle the policy date and cannot by themselves establish pre-policy exposure magnitude at 12-Oct-2023.
2. Current WITS/Comtrade retrieval is retrospective; vintage/publication timing must be preserved.
3. HS 151110 crude palm oil is not identical to NBS bottled retail vegetable oil.
4. Domestic refining/processing linkage still requires evidence.
5. The broader 2023 FX reform, naira depreciation, FX scarcity, domestic production and demand are competing mechanisms.

## Decision
Candidate C survives, but in a narrower form. The next decisive work is not post-policy retail-price scoring. It is:
- reconstruct pre-policy/monthly-or-earlier import exposure if available;
- establish crude-palm-oil → domestic processing/downstream product linkage;
- freeze a T4 scenario only after T3 evidence and counterevidence are adequate.
