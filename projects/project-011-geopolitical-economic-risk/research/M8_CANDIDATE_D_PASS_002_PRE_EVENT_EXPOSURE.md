# P003 M8 — Candidate D Pass 002: Pre-Event Refined-Fuel Exposure and Subsidy Mechanism

## Decision
**PROMOTE T2. T3 is strongly supported as a structural mechanism. HOLD T4 until the scenario target and cutoff-safe packet are frozen.**

## Pre-event trade exposure
WITS/UN Comtrade's current reconstruction of Nigeria's 2021 trade reports:
- total Nigerian imports: about USD52.068bn;
- HS271000 petroleum oils/preparations excluding crude: about USD15.731bn, the largest single HS6-level import item in the WITS summary;
- broader fuels imports: about USD16.144bn;
- major 2021 fuel suppliers included Netherlands, Belgium, Russian Federation, Norway and India.

These records establish substantial pre-event refined/fuel import exposure retrospectively. They do not by themselves establish exact data availability on 24-Feb-2022, so the replay remains R2 unless vintage/publication evidence is recovered.

## Pre-event subsidy mechanism
The mechanism is documented before the war:
- IMF material on Nigeria's 2021 subsidy re-emergence states that regulated PMS pump prices remained below the cost of imported fuel, with NNPC bearing the implicit subsidy through deductions from Federation Account oil revenues.
- The IMF estimated the 2021 implicit fuel subsidy at NGN1.912tn; estimated delivery cost about NGN233/litre versus regulated retail price about NGN167/litre.
- World Bank November 2021 material separately identified the PMS subsidy as unsustainable and a source of mounting fiscal pressure, noting that higher subsidy costs and weak crude production prevented Nigeria from fully benefiting from the oil-price boom.

Thus the core domestic transmission edge existed before the 2022 shock:
higher imported PMS/refined-product cost → larger regulated-price gap → larger subsidy/NNPC deduction → lower net oil/fiscal revenue, all else equal.

## Important refinement
Broad HS271000/refined-product exposure is not identical to PMS/gasoline exposure. The historical packet should use a narrower PMS/gasoline product line if a defensible classification can be reconstructed. Until then, the trade evidence supports the refined-fuel exposure class while the IMF/World Bank evidence supports the PMS subsidy mechanism.

## Counterchannels that must remain
- higher crude prices can increase gross export/oil revenues;
- crude production below capacity/quota weakens that benefit;
- FX affects naira import cost;
- regulated retail PMS prices move the shock from consumers toward fiscal/subsidy accounts;
- product mix within HS2710 matters;
- volumes, accounting rules, smuggling/leakage and policy decisions affect realized subsidy cost.
