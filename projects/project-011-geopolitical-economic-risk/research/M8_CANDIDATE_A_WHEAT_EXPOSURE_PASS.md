## Frozen product family for the next pass

Candidate A is narrowed from generic "food/grain shock" to **wheat**, using HS 1988/92 distinctions explicitly rather than mixing products.

Primary pre-event exposure observations for 2021:

- HS 100110 — Durum wheat: Nigeria reported world imports of about USD 2.690 billion. Ukraine accounted for about USD 491.697 million and Russia about USD 278.503 million.
- HS 100190 — Spelt, common wheat and meslin: Nigeria reported about USD 33.634 million of imports, including about USD 18.136 million from Russia.
- Ukraine exporter-reported HS 100190 exports to Nigeria were about USD 65.891 million / 260.6 million kg.

The reporter asymmetry between Nigeria-import and Ukraine-export records is itself an evidence-quality issue. These figures must not be summed as if they were one harmonized exposure series.

## Why wheat survives screening

Wheat has a measurable pre-event Nigeria exposure relationship with both Ukraine and Russia. This is stronger T2 material than the previously considered generic food category.

For HS 100110 alone, the reported 2021 partner values imply substantial shares of Nigeria's world-reported durum-wheat import value:
- Ukraine: approximately 18.3%
- Russia: approximately 10.4%
- combined: approximately 28.6%.

These percentages are descriptive trade-exposure ratios only. They do not prove domestic price pass-through.

## Proposed historical information structure

Event anchor remains 24 February 2022.

For the first replay design, **2021 trade data are candidate prior-exposure evidence**, because they precede the event. However, M8 must separately establish whether the relevant 2021 records were published/available by the chosen simulated decision cutoff. If publication timing cannot be established, the data may support retrospective reconstruction but cannot be represented as contemporaneously known evidence.

Therefore the final information cutoff is **not frozen yet**.
