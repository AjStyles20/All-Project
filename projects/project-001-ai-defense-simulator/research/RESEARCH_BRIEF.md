# Project 001 Research Brief

## Core Research Question
What architecture and interaction design can support a defensible AI-assisted presentation/defense practice system that uses user-provided materials to generate context-aware questioning and transparent feedback?

## Historical Coverage Policy
Research should cover the development of relevant ideas from approximately **1990 to the present**, and earlier where a genuinely foundational source is directly relevant.

The goal is not to collect old papers for volume. The goal is to show how the problem and enabling technologies evolved across distinct eras:
- 1990s: intelligent tutoring systems, computer-assisted instruction, early dialogue tutoring, expert systems, and speech-enabled learning where relevant.
- 2000s: natural-language tutoring, automated question generation, educational dialogue systems, automated assessment, and early presentation/speech analysis.
- 2010s: neural NLP, automatic question generation, intelligent tutoring at scale, automated feedback, learning analytics, speech and multimodal assessment, and retrieval-based educational systems.
- 2020s: transformer-based NLP, large language models, retrieval-augmented generation, generative tutoring, document-grounded dialogue, AI interview/presentation coaching, multimodal feedback, and human-AI assessment.

Recent work should not replace foundational work, and foundational work should not be treated as evidence for current capabilities when technology has materially changed.

## Research Tasks
1. Review academic work on automated question generation, retrieval-augmented generation for educational/dialogue systems, oral-exam/viva simulation, presentation coaching, automated feedback, speech-based tutoring, intelligent tutoring systems, computer-assisted instruction, and human-AI assessment.
2. Trace historical progression so later methods can be compared against earlier rule-based, statistical, neural, and generative approaches.
3. Review comparable products/systems such as presentation coaches, interview simulators, study assistants, intelligent tutors, and document-grounded conversational tools.
4. Identify what is actually novel or merely an integration of known techniques.
5. Compare candidate architectures for document ingestion, retrieval, question generation, persona control, speech input/output, session storage, and feedback.
6. Investigate evaluation methods that are feasible for a prototype without overstating educational or psychological outcomes.

## Evidence Requirements
For every significant source capture: title, author/organization, year/date, source type, URL/DOI, method, dataset/sample where applicable, results, limitations, relevance to P001, and exact claim(s) it can support.

Every source should also be tagged with a historical role when appropriate:
- Foundational
- Transitional
- Contemporary
- Current state of practice

## Research Guardrails
- Do not claim that no prior system exists unless evidence is genuinely exhaustive.
- Distinguish academic evidence from product marketing.
- Distinguish technical feasibility from validated educational effectiveness.
- Do not infer that model-generated numerical scores are objective measurements.
- Do not dismiss older research solely because it predates modern AI; foundational concepts may still explain the lineage of the system.
- Do not use older evidence to support claims about modern LLM/RAG performance unless the claim itself is technology-independent.
- Prefer historical continuity plus recent evidence, rather than recent evidence only.

## Required Outputs
- Historical literature timeline.
- Literature matrix entries.
- Comparable-system matrix.
- Candidate research-gap statement with confidence/limitations.
- Architecture evidence brief.
- Evaluation-method options.
- Handoff to Research Gap Reviewer and Architect.
