# P003 M8 — Candidate A T3 Concordance Pass

## Status
**WHEAT MECHANISM SURVIVES, BUT HISTORICAL ADMISSION REMAINS BLOCKED BY CUTOFF-SAFE DATA VINTAGE.**

## Product scope decision
Do not use HS 100110 alone as a proxy for all wheat transmission. Candidate A will use a **predeclared wheat basket**, with each HS code preserved separately and aggregation rules documented. This avoids silently treating durum wheat as equivalent to every wheat input used by Nigerian millers.

## Mechanism bridge
The defensible conceptual bridge is:

affected-origin wheat supply / world wheat shock
→ Nigerian imported unmilled wheat exposure
→ Nigerian flour millers
→ wheat flour
→ bakeries and other wheat-product manufacturers
→ bread/cereal consumer-price category

This is a mechanism hypothesis, not a causal estimate.

USDA/FAS Lagos reported in its 2022 Nigeria Grain and Feed Annual that Nigerian bakers identified foreign-exchange scarcity as a major flour-cost factor; that the Ukraine conflict had raised global wheat prices; Nigerian milling executives reported price increases; and mills could substitute/blend wheat from alternative origins. These are material counterchannels and mitigation mechanisms, not noise to remove.

## Outcome target decision
For the first historical design, the preferred outcome family is **NBS “Bread and cereals”**, rather than headline CPI.

Reason: NBS's pre-2024 CPI framework explicitly contains “Bread and cereals” under Food and Non-Alcoholic Beverages, making it substantially closer to the wheat→flour→bakery mechanism than all-items inflation.

This does not imply that all movements in Bread and cereals are caused by wheat or by the geopolitical event.

## Candidate horizon
No scoring horizon is frozen yet. It must be justified by the economic transmission lag and fixed before inspecting the post-cutoff outcome trajectory for scoring.

## Counterevidence/covariates that must be preserved
- exchange-rate/foreign-exchange scarcity;
- alternative wheat suppliers and blending/substitution;
- domestic wheat production;
- freight/logistics effects;
- inventories/contracts where historical evidence exists;
- Nigerian policy interventions;
- other cereal/input-price movements.

These factors can weaken, contest or prevent attribution of a Bread-and-cereals movement to the event pathway.

## Data-vintage blocker
UN Comtrade states that country data arrive on varying schedules, may be provisional and later revised, and that the current system retains the latest version rather than an archive of earlier versions. Data Availability supports publication-date filtering and recent-release metadata.

Therefore a current 2021 Comtrade observation cannot by itself prove what exact version was available at a February/March 2022 historical cutoff.

This is now a formal replay limitation.

## Admission decision
Candidate A is **not yet admitted** to M10.

T1: event can be authoritatively anchored.
T2: wheat exposure is retrospectively supported.
T3: an economically plausible Nigeria milling/bakery transmission mechanism is independently documented, but must still be assessed against contemporaneous counterevidence.
T4+: not released.

The next pass must either:
1. recover cutoff-safe publication/vintage evidence for the trade data; or
2. explicitly reframe the case as a retrospective reconstruction whose exposure snapshot is not claimed to have been available contemporaneously.

The second option may still support mechanism evaluation, but it cannot be mislabeled as a strict real-time historical replay.
