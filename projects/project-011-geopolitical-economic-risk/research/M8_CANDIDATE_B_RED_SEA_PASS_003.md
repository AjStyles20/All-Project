# P003 M8 — Candidate B Red Sea Pass 003: Adversarial Nigeria-Route Exposure Search

## Decision
**KEEP ON HOLD. Do not promote T2.**

The new search strengthens the global/continental shipping-disruption mechanism but still does not establish the missing Nigeria-specific route/product exposure edge required by ETEC.

## What is now strongly supported
Authoritative/institutional sources establish:
- attacks on commercial shipping beginning in late 2023;
- major carrier diversion away from the Red Sea/Suez route;
- longer voyages around the Cape of Good Hope;
- reduced Suez/Gulf of Aden traffic;
- increased transit times, capacity demand and freight costs;
- material implications for African trade and ports.

UNCTAD specifically quantifies Suez dependence for several East African economies, including Djibouti, Kenya, Tanzania and Sudan. This is useful because it demonstrates that country-specific route exposure can in principle be measured.

## What the search did NOT establish
The reviewed authoritative/institutional material did not provide a defensible equivalent Nigeria-specific measure such as:
- percentage of Nigerian foreign trade routed through Suez/Red Sea;
- identified Nigeria-bound product shipments whose pre-disruption route used Suez;
- carrier/port/customs records linking the diversion to specific Nigerian imports;
- a predeclared Nigeria shipping-exposure metric available at the historical cutoff.

The fact that vessels diverted around Africa, that African ports were affected, or that Nigeria is an importing economy does not fill this edge.

## Why generic Asia/Africa geography is insufficient
Inferring Nigeria exposure from origin geography alone would require an unverified routing assumption. A product imported from Asia is not automatically evidence that its Nigeria-bound voyage used the Red Sea/Suez route, nor that rerouting materially changed the relevant landed-cost pathway.

## ETEC state
T1 event/disruption: SUPPORTED / anchorable.
T2 Nigeria-specific exposure: INSUFFICIENT.
T3+ downstream transmission: WITHHELD.

Expected B3 output remains no stronger than event-level evidence until T2 is independently established.

## Falsification value
Candidate B remains useful as a **missing-exposure-edge stress case**, but it is not yet an admitted historical comparison case and not a negative control.

## Admission evidence that would change the decision
Promote only if one or more independently defensible records establish:
1. Nigeria-bound shipment/route use of Suez/Red Sea before disruption;
2. Nigeria-specific carrier schedule/port call/rerouting evidence;
3. customs/port/freight data tying a defined Nigerian product basket to the affected route;
4. a reproducible country-route exposure metric with historical availability.

## Research discipline
Do not use later Nigerian inflation, generic freight increases, or broad statements that Africa was affected to back-fill T2. Outcome movement cannot establish the missing exposure edge.
